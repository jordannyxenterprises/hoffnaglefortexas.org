The Full Platform

Where I Stand

Most candidates show up with slogans. I’m showing up with drafts. Three of the items below are already written as legislation — they exist because the systems they fix broke on people I know.

Families & Family Courts Taxes & Cost of Living Schools & Our Kids Public Safety & Accountability Health, Energy & Infrastructure Government That Answers to You

Families & Family Courts

This is the fight I know best, and it’s the reason I got into this race.

50/50 custody as the default starting standard

Texas should begin every custody case at equal time between two fit parents, then actually enforce the orders it hands down. A parent who follows the rules shouldn’t lose years with their children because the other side knows the system has no teeth.

  • Equal custody as the starting point, not a prize one parent has to win
  • Escalating enforcement for repeat violations of a standing order
  • Children’s sports and extracurriculars as a required consideration in custody orders
  • No child support obligation for a parent earning $50,000 a year or less

Family Court and Probate Accountability Act Drafted Bill

The people who decide where your children live should answer for how they decide it.

  • Limits on immunity for court-appointed actors — guardians ad litem, amicus attorneys, and custody evaluators
  • A State Commission on Judicial Conduct with real authority
  • Ad litem reports disclosed to both parties before the hearing, not sprung at it

Attorney Accountability and Client Protection Act Drafted Bill

Texans deserve to know who they’re hiring before they hand over their savings.

  • A public, searchable attorney grievance registry
  • Written fee agreements required for any matter over $1,000
  • Mandatory fee arbitration when a client disputes a bill
  • A civil cause of action for attorney fraud
  • An expanded Client Security Fund for people defrauded by their own lawyer

Courts that work for people without lawyers

Every Texas court — including municipal, JP, and specialty courts — should accept and use the state eFileTexas system, and should be required to accept electronic service and notice for every case and every party, including people representing themselves. Right now the paperwork alone is enough to lose a case you should have won.

Taxes & the Cost of Living

Nothing in Austin matters if people here can’t afford to stay in their homes.

Cap property taxes at what you paid

Lock property taxes to the purchase price of the home and keep them there for as long as it’s owned. No annual increases because an appraiser decided the neighborhood got expensive. The cap covers every residential property in Texas, rentals included, because when a landlord’s tax bill climbs the rent climbs with it. Reassessment happens only when a property changes hands — and a surviving spouse keeps the original rate.

A ticket shouldn’t cost you your job

Cap traffic fines at 5% of a week’s take-home pay. The same ticket that’s pocket change for one driver turns into a warrant, a suspended license, and a lost job for someone working paycheck to paycheck. The penalty should match what a person actually earns.

Make it cheaper to start a business

Cut the cost of filing a first-time LLC. The filing fee is a real barrier for someone trying to turn a side job into a business, and Texas gains nothing by keeping it high.

Turn empty offices into places people can live

Build on SB 840 and keep expanding the conversion of underused office and warehouse space into multifamily housing, aimed at moderate- and middle-income Texans. We have empty buildings and a housing shortage at the same time. That’s a problem that solves itself if the state stops standing in the way.

Schools & Our Kids

Nearly every child in this district goes to Dallas ISD. Nothing I support comes out of their schools.

Real careers, not just another test

Require high schools to accept work-study for juniors and seniors, granting credit for 10 or more hours a week in a licensed trade, construction, networking, data cabling, or computer science. Not a class about a trade — actual hours on an actual job, counting toward graduation. A kid should be able to walk out of high school with a diploma and a paycheck instead of a diploma and a loan.

Support for families who teach at home

Homeschooling parents pay the same school taxes as everyone else and save the state the full cost of a classroom seat. Texas’s education savings account program should reflect that — compensating parents for the work of educating their own children, allowing unused funds to roll over, and protecting curricular independence so state money doesn’t arrive with strings attached. This is in addition to fully funding public schools, not instead of it.

Protect kids from pornography online

Social media platforms should be required to keep pornographic content away from minors. Parents shouldn’t have to run a security operation to keep a phone from doing damage.

Guard the data schools collect on children

School contractors hold an enormous amount of information about our kids, including photographs. Texas should audit those contractors, require improperly retained student data to be deleted, and bar vendors whose leadership presents a serious child-safety conflict.

School meals worth eating

A full accounting of what our districts actually serve at lunch, what it costs, and who profits from the contracts. Parents are entitled to that answer.

Public Safety & Accountability

Good officers are hurt most by a system that protects bad ones. The cameras should point at the government, not at the people.

Body Worn Camera Transparency Act Drafted Bill

Footage belongs to the public that paid for the cameras.

  • One-year minimum retention for all body worn camera footage
  • A streamlined process for residents to request and receive footage
  • Mandatory production of footage in civil and criminal proceedings
  • Criminal and administrative penalties for unauthorized deactivation
  • A permanent bar from Texas law enforcement for any officer who commits a crime while their camera is off

Ban Flock cameras and warrantless mass tracking

Automated license plate readers and similar systems log the movements of everyone who drives past, overwhelmingly people who’ve done nothing wrong, and the records sit in private databases with almost no oversight. Texas should ban that class of surveillance outright. If law enforcement needs to track someone, they should go get a warrant.

Serious sentences for crimes against children

A mandatory 25-year minimum for an adult 21 or older who sexually abuses a child under 14, and capital eligibility for the worst of those offenses. Texas should also preserve its rule that these crimes carry no statute of limitations — a survivor who comes forward at 40 deserves the same day in court as one who comes forward at 20.

Health, Energy & Infrastructure

The things that decide whether a neighborhood grows or gets left behind.

Expand Medicaid — with a trigger that protects Texas

Texas is leaving federal health dollars on the table while our hospitals absorb the cost of treating the uninsured. Parkland carries that burden, and Dallas County property taxpayers carry Parkland. I support expanding eligibility as long as the federal match stays at or above 90%, written with an automatic reversal if that support is cut — the same safeguard nine other states already use. The deeper problem is what healthcare costs in the first place, and that fight doesn’t end with coverage.

Data centers in corridors, not neighborhoods

Texas should designate data center corridors and require large facilities to build their own power generation and secure their own water rather than drawing down the residential grid and the aquifer. If a company wants to run servers in Texas, it can generate what it burns. Families shouldn’t pay higher electric bills so a server farm can stay cool.

Protect Texas drinking water

Water is the constraint on everything Texas wants to do next. Large industrial users should report what they consume and meet efficiency standards, and residential supply should come first when there’s a shortfall.

Highways for southern Dallas. No tollways.

Build the I-35E to I-45 bypass south of the Trinity and add real capacity for the commute between southern Dallas and downtown. This district has been promised road projects for decades and paid for them twice — once in taxes and again at the toll gantry. Built once, free to drive.

Nuclear power and Texas natural gas

Expand nuclear energy programs in Texas and keep leveraging our natural gas production and export capacity. Reliable power is an economic development issue and, after 2021, a survival issue.

Stand with Texas farmers

The people who grow our food are squeezed between input costs, water access, and land pressure. Any serious Texas agenda has to include them, and I’ll keep working directly with producers on what that looks like in practice.

Government That Answers to You

The common thread in everything above: decisions should be made by people you can vote out.

Nobody voted for the THC ban

The Legislature never passed it. The Governor vetoed the bill that would have. Then unelected agencies wrote the rules anyway — and Texans started getting arrested and Texas small businesses started closing over a decision nobody cast a vote on. I’ll work to repeal the state scheduling that drives those arrests, support Texas-grown cultivation, and expand the Compassionate Use Program for patients who rely on it. Federal law on hemp is tightening on its own timeline; what Texas controls is whether Texans get arrested under rules written without a vote.

Term limits for every office in Texas

Career politicians forget who they work for. Power should rotate through the people instead of pooling in Austin.

No casinos in Dallas — and no school funding built on gambling

Casino revenue is regressive, unreliable, and a poor foundation for funding schools. Southern Dallas needs infrastructure and jobs, not a casino. I do support clarifying the legal status of fantasy sports and federally regulated prediction markets rather than leaving Texans in a gray zone, with a 21-and-over floor and real limits on advertising.

Research that helps veterans

Texas has already funded psilocybin and ibogaine research for PTSD and addiction, with support from leaders in both parties. That work should continue. Veterans who came home carrying something they can’t put down deserve every serious option.
