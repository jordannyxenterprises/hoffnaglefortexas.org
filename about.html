Meet Jordan Hoffnagle

I’m not a politician. I’m a dad, I work for a living, and on Saturday mornings you’ll find me on a field in this district trying to get eleven kids to play the same sport at the same time.

I work in the factories, restaurants, and small businesses around District 100. I know what the hours are like, what the pay is like, and what it feels like when the tax bill or the electric bill lands and the math stops working. That isn’t research to me. It’s my week.

I got into this race through the family courts. I watched how that system treats parents who follow the rules, and I learned that orders only mean something if somebody enforces them. Once you see how a system works up close — who it protects, who it doesn’t, and how little any of it has to do with your kids — you can’t un-see it. So I wrote the bill I wanted to exist. Then I wrote two more.

That’s what makes this campaign different. I’m not asking you to trust a list of values. Three pieces of legislation are already drafted: one to open up police body camera footage, one to hold family court appointees accountable, and one to protect people from their own attorneys. You can read every provision on the issues page.

District 100 runs from Oak Cliff through downtown and out east to Pleasant Grove and Casa View. It’s a lot of different neighborhoods and one shared experience: getting talked about at election time and forgotten the rest of the year. I’d rather be the guy you already know from the sidelines than one more name on a mailer.

What I’ll fight for first

Cap Property Taxes

Lock your property tax to what you paid for your home. It covers rentals too — when the tax bill goes up, so does the rent.

50/50 Custody

Equal custody as the default for two fit parents, with enforcement that has teeth.

Real Careers, Not Just a Test

Credit for real hours in a real trade, so kids graduate with a paycheck instead of a loan.

Data Centers in Corridors

Built where they belong, generating their own power — not next door and not on your electric bill.

Body Cameras On, Flock Cameras Off

Footage retained and accessible. Cameras pointed at the government, not at you.

Nobody Voted for the THC Ban

Unelected agencies wrote rules that put Texans in handcuffs. That’s not how law is supposed to work.

That’s the short list. The full platform — Medicaid, housing, water, roads, term limits, and the three drafted bills — is on the issues page.
